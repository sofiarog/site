Research Question

Carrying capacity, a concept originating in ecology, is often understood as a dynamic limit arising from the relationship between finite resources and those who consume them (Del Monte-Luna et al., 2024). Characterised as the maximum pressure a space can withstand without its ecosystems degrading, this conception underpins many approaches to the territorialisation of the planetary boundaries framework. It assumes, indeed, that this capacity can be measured from the outside, through flows of matter and energy crossing the territory. However, territories are open spaces: they import resources and export their pressures to other spaces (Arrow et al., 1996), so that their "capacity" cannot be read solely in their biophysics; it depends on social dynamics — such as modes of production and consumption, inequalities, and land uses (Świąder, Szewrański & Kazak, 2020) — and on governance arrangements — such as competencies or decision-making scales — that partly exceed the boundaries of the territory under consideration (Abernethy, 2001; Newell & Cousins, 2015). What, then, makes a carrying capacity "endogenous", if it is produced by flows and power relations that exceed the territory?

This thesis proposes to focus in particular on the planetary boundaries framework and its territorialisations, and is organised in three parts. The first departs from the concept of "limits" to understand how it is apprehended in the economic tradition, and how it has been transformed from the 1970s to the present day, offering a comparative analysis of the "limits" of Meadows et al. (1972) with those of Rockström et al. (2009) and Caesar et al. (2024). These chapters draw in part on the work devoted to carrying capacity (see for example Del Monte-Luna et al., 2024; Abernethy, 2001). The second part seeks to combine a systematic bibliometric analysis of the different ways of territorialising the planetary boundaries framework with a multicriteria analysis grid drawing on the concepts of ecological economics to assess territorialisation methods, whether they fall under the urban planetary boundaries approach (Hoornweg et al., 2016), the ecological footprint and biocapacity (Wackernagel, 1994; Świąder et al., 2020), or territorial metabolism and its extensions beyond urban boundaries (Newell et Cousins, 2015; Kronenberg et al., 2024; Marom, 2026). It then develops a typology of territorial limits supported by case studies, integrating work on situated socio-ecological limits (Savoldi & Soriani, 2026).

The third and final part applies this work to the Hauts-de-France region, exploring ecological thresholds and their implications for public action.

Keywords: planetary boundaries · ecological limits · sustainability science · ecological economics · carrying capacity · metabolism.
