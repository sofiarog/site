Ph.D. & research in few words...

Research Question

The Planetary Boundaries Framework (Rockström et al., 2009; Steffen et al., 2015; Richardson et al., 2023; Caesar et al., 2024) defines nine global limits, but public action happens in regions, cities and rural areas. My thesis questions how these limits can be transposed to territories while taking into account the intrinsic ecological thresholds of each space.

Rather than simply downscaling the nine boundaries, I propose a bottom-up approach to identifying endogenous carrying capacities. It rests on a systemic framework linking biophysical metabolisms, social dynamics and governance arrangements.

This thesis examines the territorialisations of the planetary boundaries framework. It is organised in three parts. The first builds a conceptual architecture of “limits” in economic traditions and traces their transformation from Meadows to Rockström, from the register of forecasting to that of precaution, drawing in particular on work on carrying capacity and its genealogy as a political technology (Del Monte-Luna et al., 2004; Abernethy, 2001; Kluzik, 2026). The second combines a systematic bibliometric analysis with a multicriteria grid to assess territorialisation methods, whether they stem from the urban approach to planetary boundaries (Hoornweg et al., 2016), from ecological footprint and biocapacity (Wackernagel, 1994; Świąder et al., 2020), or from territorial metabolism and its extensions beyond urban boundaries (Newell & Cousins, 2015; Kronenberg et al., 2024; Marom, 2026). It then develops a typology of territorial limits, supported by case studies, that incorporates work on situated socio-ecological limits (Savoldi & Soriani, 2026). The third applies this work to the Hauts-de-France region, exploring the relevant ecological thresholds and their implications for public action.

Keywords: planetary boundaries · ecological limits · sustainability science · ecological economics · carrying capacity · metabolism.
