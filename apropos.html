About me

Professional page LinkedIn Google Scholar

I'm a Ph.D. student in Ecological Economics at the University of Lille. My thesis, Les limites planétaires territoriales (Territorial Planetary Boundaries), questions how limits designed at the global scale can be translated to territories, taking into account the ecological thresholds specific to each place. My thesis is supervised by Florence Jany-Catrice and Grégory Salle, and is funded by the University of Lille and the Hauts-de-France Region.

Rather than simply downscaling the nine planetary boundaries, I develop a bottom-up approach to identifying endogenous carrying capacities, linking biophysical metabolisms, social dynamics and governance arrangements. The work traces how the idea of “limits” has evolved from Meadows to Rockström, combines a systematic bibliometric analysis with a multicriteria grid to assess territorialisation methods, and builds a typology of territorial limits. The final application focuses on the Hauts-de-France region and its implications for public action. More details on theses.fr.

My university studies span languages, law and economics: a B.A. in Languages and Literature (Serbian-Croatian) at Sorbonne Paris IV, a B.A. in Public Law, and an M.Sc. in Economic Analysis and Policy at the University of Strasbourg, followed by a second master's in Development Economics at the University of Grenoble Alpes.

Before starting my Ph.D., I worked in French public administration. At the Ministry of Ecological Transition, I was a Research Officer in the Research and Innovation Department, where I helped organise events for the French Presidency of the Council of the European Union, including one on sustainability science. At the Ministry of Agriculture, I was a Forward Studies Officer in the Human Resources Department, conducting foresight research and preparing briefing notes for decision-makers. Earlier in my career, I also worked in health and pension management at Gras Savoye Willis Towers Watson.

During my studies, I wrote three research papers. The first examines the European Union facing planetary boundaries; the second, building sustainable European cities by integrating the systemic approach of sustainability science to address social and environmental challenges; the third, a critical analysis of accounting foundations in light of Karl Polanyi's theory, moving towards a re-evaluation of economic measurement. Some of these documents are available on ResearchGate.
